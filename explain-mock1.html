第 1 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

必定為真

重點：連續推理：由「有些」找出一個具體的人，再一步步用「所有」和「沒有」推下去；要說明一句不一定為真，就構造一個所有條件都成立、但這句不成立的例子。

某校知道三件事：(1) 所有參加編程隊的同學都有參加數學隊；(2) 沒有中一學生參加數學隊；(3) 有些參加辯論隊的同學也參加了編程隊。以下哪一項必定為真？

  1. A沒有中一學生參加辯論隊。
  2. B有些參加數學隊的同學沒有參加編程隊。
  3. C有些參加辯論隊的同學不是中一學生。✓
  4. D所有參加辯論隊的同學都有參加數學隊。

答案與解說

答案：C

由 (3)，有一位同學 X 同時在辯論隊和編程隊；由 (1)，X 也在數學隊；由 (2)，X 不是中一學生。所以「有些參加辯論隊的同學不是中一學生」必定為真，X 就是例子。其他三項都有反例：假設全校只有兩位同學，甲在辯論隊、編程隊和數學隊，不是中一學生；乙是中一學生，只參加辯論隊。三件事都成立，但 A（乙是中一學生，在辯論隊）、B（數學隊只有甲，他也在編程隊）、D（乙不在數學隊）都不成立。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A第 (2) 件事只說數學隊沒有中一學生。辯論隊可以有不在數學隊（也就不在編程隊）的中一學生。
  • B數學隊的同學可能全部都在編程隊（例如兩隊的成員完全相同），這句就不成立。
  • D第 (3) 件事只說「有些」辯論隊同學在編程隊；其他辯論隊同學可以不在數學隊。

為何會考

逆否命題和「必定為真」在 16 屆裏分別有 12 屆和 6 屆考過（難點庫 M01、M02）；2025/26 初級卷一甲部也有「必定為真」的題目。

出題說明（給老師）

中等題：要連用三件事。錯誤選項分別是把「沒有」用錯對象（A）、忽略兩隊可以相同（B）、把「有些」當成「所有」（D）；程式列舉了最多 3 位同學的所有情況（每人 4 個屬性），只有 C 每種情況都成立。

第 2 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

把條件取反

重點：!(P && Q) = !P || !Q，!(P || Q) = !P && !Q，每一層都要換；> 的相反是 <=，== 的相反是 !=；&& 比 || 先計算。

以下哪一個條件，對所有整數 x 和 y 都和 !(x > 3 && (y <= 5 || x == y)) 的值相同？

  1. Ax <= 3 && (y > 5 || x != y)
  2. Bx <= 3 || y > 5 && x != y✓
  3. C(x <= 3 || y > 5) && x != y
  4. Dx <= 3 || y > 5 || x != y

答案與解說

答案：B

用兩次德摩根定律：!(P && Q) = !P || !Q，而 Q 是 y <= 5 || x == y，所以 !Q = y > 5 && x != y。合起來是 x <= 3 || (y > 5 && x != y)；因為 && 比 || 先計算，括號可以省去，就是選項 B。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A兩層的 && 和 || 都沒有換：外層的 && 應變成 ||，內層的 || 應變成 &&。例如 x = 0、y = 0：原式是真，這個條件是假。
  • C括號放錯了位置：&& 比 || 先計算，正確的式子是 x <= 3 || (y > 5 && x != y)。例如 x = 0、y = 0：原式是真，這個條件是假。
  • D只換了外層：內層的 y <= 5 || x == y 取反後應是 y > 5 && x != y，|| 要變成 &&。例如 x = 4、y = 0：原式是假，這個條件是真。

為何會考

判斷布爾式是否等價在 16 屆裏有 10 屆考過（難點庫 M03）；&& 先於 || 有 6 屆（L10）。

出題說明（給老師）

中等題：要在兩層用德摩根定律，再判斷沒有括號時的優先次序。程式用 x、y 由 −10 至 10 的所有組合核對，只有 B 每一組都和原式相同；三個錯誤選項的反例也由程式核對。

第 3 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

隊列：奇數輸出，偶數減半放回

重點：隊列先進先出：新放入的數排在最後，要等前面的數全部取出後才輪到它。追蹤時每一步都寫下整個隊列。

把 1、2、3、4、5、6 依次放入一個隊列（先放入的先取出）。然後重複以下步驟，直至隊列變空：由隊頭取出一個數 v；如果 v 是奇數，輸出 v；否則把 v ÷ 2 放入隊尾。輸出的次序是甚麼？

  1. A1 3 5 1 3 1✓
  2. B1 1 3 5 3 1
  3. C3 5 1 3 1 1
  4. D1 1 3 1 5 3

答案與解說

答案：A

逐步寫下隊列（左邊是隊頭）：1 2 3 4 5 6 → 取 1，輸出 → 取 2，放入 1：3 4 5 6 1 → 取 3，輸出 → 取 4，放入 2：5 6 1 2 → 取 5，輸出 → 取 6，放入 3：1 2 3 → 取 1，輸出 → 取 2，放入 1：3 1 → 取 3，輸出 → 取 1，輸出。輸出 1 3 5 1 3 1。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • B以為偶數減半後馬上再判斷，是奇數就立即輸出。其實 v ÷ 2 要放到隊尾排隊，等前面的數都取出後才輪到它。
  • C當成了堆疊：每次取出最後放入的數。隊列是先放入的先取出。
  • D把 v ÷ 2 放回隊頭（於是下一步馬上取出它），不是放到隊尾。

為何會考

堆疊與隊列的操作追蹤在 16 屆裏有 10 屆考過（難點庫 R08）；2025/26 初級卷一甲部有兩題考隊列和堆疊。

出題說明（給老師）

容易題。三個錯誤選項分別來自當成堆疊、放回隊頭、減半後立即判斷，每個都由改了規則的模擬程式算出。

第 4 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

堆疊：哪些取出次序可能出現？

重點：堆疊的取出次序：某數取出之後，比它小而未取出的數必須由大到小取出。

把 1、2、3、4、5 依次放入一個堆疊，中途可以隨時取出（取出的總是最後放入而還未取出的數）。全部取出後，記下取出的次序。以下哪些次序可能出現？

  1. I.3 2 5 4 1
  2. II.3 1 2 4 5
  3. III.2 1 5 4 3
  4. IV.1 4 2 3 5
  5. V.1 3 2 5 4
  1. A只有 I、III、IV 和 V
  2. B只有 I 和 III
  3. C只有 I、III 和 V✓
  4. D只有 I、II、III 和 V

答案與解說

答案：C

判斷方法：某個數 x 取出時，比 x 小而還未取出的數都在堆疊裏，之後必須由大到小取出。I（3 2 5 4 1）：放 1、2、3，取 3、2；放 4、5，取 5、4；再取 1，可以。II（3 1 2 4 5）：取出 3 後 1、2 在堆疊裏，下一個卻是 1，不可以。III（2 1 5 4 3）：放 1、2，取 2、1；放 3、4、5，取 5、4、3，可以。IV（1 4 2 3 5）：取出 4 後 2、3 在堆疊裏，下一個卻是 2，不可以。V（1 3 2 5 4）：可以。所以只有 I、III 和 V。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A以為 IV「1 4 2 3 5」可以。取出 4 時，2 和 3 已經放入而未取出，3 在 2 上面，所以 2 不能在 3 之前取出。
  • B以為 V「1 3 2 5 4」不可以。其實可以：放 1 取 1；放 2、3，取 3、2；放 4、5，取 5、4。
  • D以為 II「3 1 2 4 5」可以。取出 3 時，1 和 2 都在堆疊裏，2 在 1 上面，所以 1 不能在 2 之前取出。

為何會考

由取出結果判斷堆疊、隊列的性質，16 屆裏有 12 屆考過（難點庫 R09）。

出題說明（給老師）

由取出結果判斷堆疊、隊列的性質，在 16 屆裏有 12 屆考過（難點庫 R09；2025/26 初級卷一也考過由 pop 結果推斷）。精選 30 題比較同一串操作用堆疊和隊列的取出次序；這題要逐一判斷五個次序是否可能，三個錯誤選項各錯判一個。答案用程式模擬五個次序核對過。

第 5 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

數字由左至右不遞增的四位數

重點：「各位數字不遞增或不遞減」：只要數「選哪幾個數字（可以重複）」；可以按有幾個不同的數字分類，再留意 0 和首位的限制。

四位數（1000 至 9999）之中，有多少個的各位數字由左至右不遞增（每一位都不大於前一位，可以相等）？例如 9530、9533、7777 都是，9354 不是。

  1. A210
  2. B715
  3. C495
  4. D714✓

答案與解說

答案：D

每一個合乎要求的數，都由「選哪 4 個數字（可以重複）」決定：把選出的數字由大到小排好，就是唯一的寫法。按有幾個不同的數字分類：4 個不同，C(10, 4) = 210；3 個不同（其中一個用兩次），C(10, 3) × 3 = 360；2 個不同（用法有 3 + 1、2 + 2、1 + 3 三種），C(10, 2) × 3 = 135；全部相同，10。合共 210 + 360 + 135 + 10 = 715。（也可以用隔板法：4 個數字和 9 塊隔板排成一行，C(13, 4) = 715。）其中只有 0000 不是四位數（最大的數字是 0 時，四位都是 0），所以答案是 714。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A只數了嚴格遞減（四個數字各不相同）的數。題目容許相等，例如 9533、7777 也要數。
  • B數對了「由 0 至 9 選 4 個可以重複的數字」的方法，但忘了 0000 不是四位數。
  • C以為不能用 0，只由 1 至 9 選（C(12, 4) = 495），漏了 9530、5000 這類以 0 結尾的數。

為何會考

排列組合在 16 屆裏有 12 屆考過（難點庫 M09），2023/24 有 4 題、2025/26 有 3 題。

出題說明（給老師）

中等至難題（比真卷難）：精選 30 題考過「嚴格遞增是 C(n, k)」，這題容許相等，要數可以重複的組合，還要扣去 0000。答案用程式數過 1000 至 9999 每個數；三個錯誤選項分別是只數嚴格遞減、忘了扣 0000、以為不能用 0。

第 6 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

A 在 B 前，C、D 在 B 後

重點：只關心某幾個人的先後次序時，可以忽略其他人：k 個人的 k! 種先後次序機會相同，數出合乎要求的次序就可以。

A、B、C、D、E 五人隨機排成一行，每一種排法的機會相同。A 排在 B 前面，而且 C 和 D 都排在 B 後面（都不一定相鄰）的概率是多少？

  1. A1/8
  2. B1/12✓
  3. C1/24
  4. D1/4

答案與解說

答案：B

只看 A、B、C、D 四人的先後次序：E 站在哪裏都不影響這四人之間的次序，而 4! = 24 種先後次序機會相同。合乎要求的次序：B 前面只有 A，後面是 C 和 D，即 A B C D 或 A B D C，共 2 種，所以概率是 2/24 = 1/12。（核對：120 種排法之中剛好有 10 種合乎要求。）

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A以為「A 在 B 前」「C 在 B 後」「D 在 B 後」各有一半機會而且互不影響，所以是 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2。其實三件事都和 B 的位置有關，不是互不影響的。
  • C以為 C、D 的先後也是固定的（只有 A B C D 一種）。C 和 D 都在 B 後面就可以，A B D C 也合乎要求。
  • D只計算了「B 在四人之中排第二」的機會。B 前面那一位還要是 A，不能是 C 或 D。

為何會考

等可能概率在 16 屆裏有 8 屆考過（難點庫 M14），近 4 屆每屆都有（2023/24 有 4 題）。

出題說明（給老師）

中等題。用「只看相關的對象」一步得答案；三個錯誤選項分別是當成互不影響、把 C、D 的次序也固定、漏了 B 前面要是 A。答案用程式列出 120 種排法核對。

第 7 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

每步走 (1, 2) 或 (2, 1)

重點：找不變量：這裏每一步 x + y 都增加 3，所以 x + y 除以 3 的餘數不變（一直是 0），這樣就能排除很多點；再用「每步最多、最少」定出範圍。

一枚棋子由 (0, 0) 出發，每一步可以由 (x, y) 走到 (x + 1, y + 2) 或 (x + 2, y + 1)。以下哪一點是棋子可以到達的？

  1. A(100, 100)
  2. B(2, 70)
  3. C(99, 100)
  4. D(50, 52)✓

答案與解說

答案：D

每一步 x + y 都增加 3，所以走了 k 步後 x + y = 3k；而且每一步 x、y 各最少加 1、最多加 2，所以 k ≤ x ≤ 2k、k ≤ y ≤ 2k。(50, 52)：x + y = 102，k = 34，而 34 ≤ 50 ≤ 68、34 ≤ 52 ≤ 68：走 (1, 2) 18 次、(2, 1) 16 次，得 (18 + 32, 36 + 16) = (50, 52)，可以到達。(2, 70)：k = 24，但 x = 2 < 24，不可能。(100, 100) 和 (99, 100)：x + y 不是 3 的倍數。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A每一步 x + y 都增加 3，所以能到達的點 x + y 必定是 3 的倍數；100 + 100 = 200 不是。
  • B2 + 70 = 72 是 3 的倍數，要走 24 步；但每一步 x 最少加 1，24 步後 x 至少是 24，不可能是 2。
  • C99 + 100 = 199 不是 3 的倍數。

為何會考

不變的量與奇偶在 16 屆裏有 6 屆考過（難點庫 M16），2025/26 初級卷一甲部有兩題；網格與距離有 8 屆（M24）。

出題說明（給老師）

中等題。兩個錯誤選項只要看 x + y 就能排除，(2, 70) 要再想一步範圍；答案用程式由 (0, 0) 搜尋所有可到達的點核對。

第 8 題初級組及高級組單選題1 分 · 建議 2:00

插入排序比較了多少次？

重點：插入排序的比較次數 = 移動次數（逆序對的數目）+ 沒有移到最前面的次數。

以下插入排序把 a 由小到大排好。程式輸出甚麼？

int a[7] = {5, 2, 8, 1, 9, 3, 7}, cmp = 0;
for (int i = 1; i < 7; i++) {
    int x = a[i], j = i - 1;
    while (j >= 0) {
        cmp++;
        if (a[j] > x) { a[j + 1] = a[j]; j--; }
        else break;
    }
    a[j + 1] = x;
}
cout << cmp;
  1. A9
  2. B13✓
  3. C15
  4. D21

答案與解說

答案：B

每個數向左比較：比它大的就向右移（比較一次、移動一次），遇到不比它大的就停（再比較一次）；移到最前面就不用再比較。i = 1（2）：比 5，移動，到了最前面，1 次。i = 2（8）：比 5，停，1 次。i = 3（1）：比 8、5、2，全部移動，到了最前面，3 次。i = 4（9）：比 8，停，1 次。i = 5（3）：比 9、8、5，移動，比 2，停，4 次。i = 6（7）：比 9、8，移動，比 5，停，3 次。合共 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 3 = 13。

錯誤選項錯在哪裏

  • A只數了移動的次數（即逆序對的數目）。每次遇到不比 x 大的數而停下，也比較了一次。
  • C以為每個數最後都要多比較一次才停下：9 + 6。其實移到最前面（j 變成 -1）的數不用再比較，2 和 1 就是這樣。
  • D以為每一對數都要比較一次：6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1。這是最壞的情況，這裏遇到不比 x 大的數就會停下。

為何會考

某地的硬幣只有 1 元、3 元和 4 元三種。要付 n 元時，「貪心法」每次都用不超過剩餘金額的最大硬幣，直至付清。例如付 7 元：先用 4 元，剩 3 元，再用 3 元，共 2 枚。（a）用貪心法付 18 元，要用 I 枚硬幣。求 I。（b）付 18 元最少要用 J1 枚硬幣；1 至 100 元之中，有 J2 個金額用貪心法得到的硬幣數目不是最少的。求 J1 和 J2。

每格只填一個整數。J1、J2 都正確才得 J 的 2 分。

答案與解說

答案：I：6；J1：5；J2：24

（a）18 = 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 1 + 1，貪心法用 6 枚，I = 6。（b）18 = 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 只用 5 枚；4 枚最多只有 16 元，不夠，所以 J1 = 5。先試小的金額找規律。設 n = 4q + r，r 是 n 除以 4 的餘數。貪心法先用 q 枚 4 元，再付餘下的 r 元：r = 0 時不用再付，r = 1 時一枚 1 元，r = 3 時一枚 3 元，r = 2 時要兩枚 1 元。用 q 枚或更少的硬幣最多只能付 4q 元，所以 r = 1、3 時 q + 1 枚已是最少，r = 0 時 q 枚也是最少。只有 r = 2 而且 q ≥ 1 時可以更好：把一枚 4 元和兩枚 1 元（共 6 元）換成兩枚 3 元，只用 q + 1 枚。所以不是最少的金額是 6、10、14、…、98（除以 4 餘 2，而且不小於 6），共 (98 − 6) ÷ 4 + 1 = 24 個，J2 = 24。

常見錯誤答案

  • I = 5：這是最少的枚數，不是貪心法的結果。貪心法先用四枚 4 元（16 元），剩 2 元只能用兩枚 1 元，共 6 枚。
  • J2 = 25：把 2 元也算了。付 2 元用貪心法是 1 + 1，已經是最少；由 6 元起，每隔 4 元才出現一次：6、10、…、98，共 24 個。
  • J2 = 1：只找到 6 元這一個反例。其實 10、14、18 等都是：把一枚 4 元和兩枚 1 元換成兩枚 3 元，就少用一枚。

Must be true

Key point: Chain the facts: from "some" pick one concrete person, then apply the "every" and "no" facts step by step. To show a statement need not be true, build an example where all the facts hold but the statement fails.

A school knows three facts: (1) every student in the programming team is also in the maths team; (2) no Form 1 student is in the maths team; (3) some students in the debating team are also in the programming team. Which of the following must be true?

  1. ANo Form 1 student is in the debating team.
  2. BSome students in the maths team are not in the programming team.
  3. CSome students in the debating team are not Form 1 students.✓
  4. DEvery student in the debating team is in the maths team.

Answer and explanation

Answer: C

By (3), some student X is in both the debating and programming teams; by (1), X is also in the maths team; by (2), X is not a Form 1 student. So "some students in the debating team are not Form 1 students" must be true, with X as the example. The other three have counterexamples: suppose the school has just two students. P is in the debating, programming and maths teams and is not in Form 1; Q is a Form 1 student only in the debating team. All three facts hold, but A (Q is a Form 1 debater), B (the maths team is just P, who is in the programming team) and D (Q is not in the maths team) are all false.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • AFact (2) only keeps Form 1 students out of the maths team. The debating team can have Form 1 students who are not in the maths team, and so not in the programming team.
  • BEveryone in the maths team might be in the programming team (for example if the two teams have the same members), and then this is false.
  • DFact (3) says only that some debaters are in the programming team; other debaters need not be in the maths team.

Why it is tested

The contrapositive and "must be true" appeared in 12 and 6 of 16 years (bank items M01, M02); the 2025/26 Junior Paper 1 Section A also had "must be true" questions.

Notes for teachers

A medium question that needs all three facts in a row. The wrong options apply "no" to the wrong team (A), forget the two teams could be the same (B), and read "some" as "all" (D). A program listed every school of up to 3 students (4 properties each); only C holds in all of them.

Q2Junior and SeniorSingle answer1 marks · suggested 2:00

Negating a condition

Key point: !(P && Q) = !P || !Q and !(P || Q) = !P && !Q, at every level; the opposite of > is <= and of == is !=; && is evaluated before ||.

Which condition has the same value as !(x > 3 && (y <= 5 || x == y)) for all integers x and y?

  1. Ax <= 3 && (y > 5 || x != y)
  2. Bx <= 3 || y > 5 && x != y✓
  3. C(x <= 3 || y > 5) && x != y
  4. Dx <= 3 || y > 5 || x != y

Answer and explanation

Answer: B

Apply De Morgan's law twice: !(P && Q) = !P || !Q, and with Q = y <= 5 || x == y, !Q = y > 5 && x != y. Together this is x <= 3 || (y > 5 && x != y); since && is evaluated before ||, the brackets can be dropped, giving option B.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • ANeither connective was switched: the outer && should become || and the inner || should become &&. At x = 0, y = 0 the original is true but this is false.
  • CThe brackets are in the wrong place: && is evaluated before ||, so the correct form is x <= 3 || (y > 5 && x != y). At x = 0, y = 0 the original is true but this is false.
  • DSwitches only the outer level: the inner y <= 5 || x == y becomes y > 5 && x != y, so its || must turn into &&. At x = 4, y = 0 the original is false but this is true.

Why it is tested

Boolean equivalence appeared in 10 of 16 years (bank item M03), and && before || in 6 (L10).

Notes for teachers

A medium question: De Morgan's law at two levels, then precedence without brackets. A program checked every x and y from −10 to 10, and only B matches the original everywhere; the counterexamples for the wrong options were checked the same way.

Q3Junior and SeniorSingle answer1 marks · suggested 2:00

Queue: print odd numbers, halve even ones

Key point: A queue is first in, first out: a newly added number goes to the back and waits until everything ahead of it is taken. When tracing, write down the whole queue at every step.

Put 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 into a queue in that order (first in, first out). Then repeat until the queue is empty: take the number v at the front; if v is odd, print v; otherwise put v ÷ 2 at the back. In what order are the numbers printed?

  1. A1 3 5 1 3 1✓
  2. B1 1 3 5 3 1
  3. C3 5 1 3 1 1
  4. D1 1 3 1 5 3

Answer and explanation

Answer: A

Write the queue at each step (front on the left): 1 2 3 4 5 6 → take 1, print → take 2, add 1: 3 4 5 6 1 → take 3, print → take 4, add 2: 5 6 1 2 → take 5, print → take 6, add 3: 1 2 3 → take 1, print → take 2, add 1: 3 1 → take 3, print → take 1, print. The output is 1 3 5 1 3 1.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • BTreats the halved number as checked at once and printed straight away if odd. In fact v ÷ 2 joins the back of the queue and waits for everything ahead of it.
  • CTreats it as a stack, taking the most recently added number each time. A queue takes the earliest one.
  • DPuts v ÷ 2 back at the front (so it is taken next), not at the back.

Why it is tested

Tracing stacks and queues appeared in 10 of 16 years (bank item R08); the 2025/26 Junior Paper 1 Section A had two questions on queues and stacks.

Notes for teachers

An easy question. The wrong options come from using a stack, putting the number back at the front, and checking the halved number at once; each was produced by a simulation with that rule changed.

Q4Junior and SeniorSingle answer1 marks · suggested 2:00

Stack: which pop orders are possible?

Key point: For pop orders of a stack: after a number is popped, the smaller numbers not yet popped must come out from largest to smallest.

The numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 are pushed onto a stack in that order, and numbers may be popped at any time (a pop always takes the last number pushed that has not been popped yet). After all have been popped, the pop order is recorded. Which of these orders are possible?

  1. I.3 2 5 4 1
  2. II.3 1 2 4 5
  3. III.2 1 5 4 3
  4. IV.1 4 2 3 5
  5. V.1 3 2 5 4
  1. AI, III, IV and V only
  2. BI and III only
  3. CI, III and V only✓
  4. DI, II, III and V only

Answer and explanation

Answer: C

The test: when a number x is popped, the smaller numbers not yet popped are all on the stack and must come out from largest to smallest afterwards. I (3 2 5 4 1): push 1, 2, 3, pop 3, 2; push 4, 5, pop 5, 4; pop 1. Possible. II (3 1 2 4 5): after 3 is popped, 1 and 2 are on the stack, yet the next pop is 1. Impossible. III (2 1 5 4 3): push 1, 2, pop 2, 1; push 3, 4, 5, pop 5, 4, 3. Possible. IV (1 4 2 3 5): after 4 is popped, 2 and 3 are on the stack, yet the next pop is 2. Impossible. V (1 3 2 5 4): possible. So the answer is I, III and V only.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • AThinks IV, 1 4 2 3 5, is possible. When 4 is popped, 2 and 3 are already on the stack with 3 above 2, so 2 cannot come out before 3.
  • BThinks V, 1 3 2 5 4, is impossible. It works: push 1, pop 1; push 2 and 3, pop 3 and 2; push 4 and 5, pop 5 and 4.
  • DThinks II, 3 1 2 4 5, is possible. When 3 is popped, 1 and 2 are both on the stack with 2 above 1, so 1 cannot come out before 2.

Why it is tested

Judging stack and queue properties from pop results appeared in 12 of 16 years (bank item R09).

Notes for teachers

Judging stack and queue properties from pop results appeared in 12 of 16 years (bank item R09; the 2025/26 Junior Paper 1 also asked what pop results imply). 精選 30 題 compared the pop orders of one sequence of operations on a stack and a queue; here students must decide whether each of five orders is possible, and each wrong option misjudges exactly one. Four-digit numbers whose digits never increase

Key point: For digits that never increase (or never decrease), count only which digits are used, repeats allowed; split by how many different digits there are, and watch out for 0 and the first digit.

How many four-digit numbers (1000 to 9999) have digits that never increase from left to right (each digit is at most the one before it; equal is allowed)? For example 9530, 9533 and 7777 do, but 9354 does not.

  1. A210
  2. B715
  3. C495
  4. D714✓

Answer and explanation

Answer: D

Each valid number is fixed by which 4 digits it uses (repeats allowed): written from largest to smallest, there is only one order. Sort by the number of different digits: 4 different, C(10, 4) = 210; 3 different with one used twice, C(10, 3) × 3 = 360; 2 different (used 3 + 1, 2 + 2 or 1 + 3 times), C(10, 2) × 3 = 135; all the same, 10. In total 210 + 360 + 135 + 10 = 715. (Or with stars and bars: 4 digits and 9 dividers in a row, C(13, 4) = 715.) Only 0000 is not a four-digit number (if the largest digit is 0, all four are 0), so the answer is 714.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • ACounts only strictly decreasing numbers (four different digits). Equal digits are allowed, so 9533 and 7777 count too.
  • BCorrectly counts the ways to choose 4 digits from 0 to 9 with repetition, but forgets that 0000 is not a four-digit number.
  • CAssumes 0 cannot be used and chooses only from 1 to 9 (C(12, 4) = 495), missing numbers ending in 0 such as 9530 and 5000.

Why it is tested

A before B, with C and D after B

Key point: When only the order of a few people matters, ignore everyone else: the k! orders of k people are equally likely, so just count the good ones.

A, B, C, D and E stand in a random line, every order equally likely. What is the probability that A is somewhere before B, and C and D are both somewhere after B (not necessarily next to each other)?

  1. A1/8
  2. B1/12✓
  3. C1/24
  4. D1/4

Answer and explanation

Answer: B

Look only at the order of A, B, C and D: where E stands does not affect it, and the 4! = 24 orders of the four are equally likely. The good orders have only A before B, and C and D after it: A B C D or A B D C, 2 in all, so the probability is 2/24 = 1/12. (Check: exactly 10 of the 120 lines work.)

  • CAssumes C and D must also be in a fixed order (only A B C D). Both after B is enough, so A B D C also works.
  • DFinds only the chance that B is second among the four. The one before B must also be A, not C or D.

A medium question. Looking only at the relevant people gives the answer in one step; the wrong options come from assuming independence, fixing the order of C and D, and forgetting that the one before B must be A. The answer was checked by listing all 120 lines.

Each step moves (1, 2) or (2, 1)

Key point: Look for an invariant: here every step adds 3 to x + y, so the remainder of x + y divided by 3 never changes (it stays 0), which rules out many points at once; then use the most and least per step to bound the rest.

A piece starts at (0, 0). In each step it moves from (x, y) to (x + 1, y + 2) or (x + 2, y + 1). Which of these points can it reach?

  1. A(100, 100)
  2. B(2, 70)
  3. C(99, 100)
  4. D(50, 52)✓

Answer and explanation

Answer: D

Every step adds 3 to x + y, so after k steps x + y = 3k; and each step adds between 1 and 2 to each of x and y, so k ≤ x ≤ 2k and k ≤ y ≤ 2k. (50, 52): x + y = 102, k = 34, and 34 ≤ 50 ≤ 68, 34 ≤ 52 ≤ 68: 18 steps of (1, 2) and 16 of (2, 1) give (18 + 32, 36 + 16) = (50, 52), so it is reachable. (2, 70): k = 24 but x = 2 < 24, impossible. (100, 100) and (99, 100): x + y is not a multiple of 3.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • AEvery step adds 3 to x + y, so any reachable point has x + y a multiple of 3; 100 + 100 = 200 is not.
  • B2 + 70 = 72 is a multiple of 3, which needs 24 steps; but each step adds at least 1 to x, so after 24 steps x is at least 24, not 2.
  • C99 + 100 = 199 is not a multiple of 3.

Why it is tested

Invariants and parity appeared in 6 of 16 years (bank item M16), with two questions in the 2025/26 Junior Paper 1 Section A; grids and distances appeared in 8 (M24).

Notes for teachers

A medium question. Two wrong options fall to x + y alone, while (2, 70) needs the extra step about bounds; the answer was checked by a program that searches every reachable point from (0, 0).

How many comparisons does insertion sort make?

Key point: Insertion sort's comparisons = moves (the number of inversions) + the number of times a value does not reach the front.

This insertion sort puts a into increasing order. What does the program print?

int a[7] = {5, 2, 8, 1, 9, 3, 7}, cmp = 0;
for (int i = 1; i < 7; i++) {
    int x = a[i], j = i - 1;
    while (j >= 0) {
        cmp++;
        if (a[j] > x) { a[j + 1] = a[j]; j--; }
        else break;
    }
    a[j + 1] = x;
}
cout << cmp;
  1. A9
  2. B13✓
  3. C15
  4. D21

Answer and explanation

Answer: B

Each number is compared leftwards: a larger number moves right (one comparison, one move), and a number not larger stops it (one more comparison); a number that reaches the front needs no further comparison. i = 1 (2): compare with 5, move, front reached: 1. i = 2 (8): compare with 5, stop: 1. i = 3 (1): compare with 8, 5, 2, all move, front reached: 3. i = 4 (9): compare with 8, stop: 1. i = 5 (3): compare with 9, 8, 5, moving them, then 2, stop: 4. i = 6 (7): compare with 9, 8, moving them, then 5, stop: 3. Total 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 3 = 13.

  • CThinks every number needs one extra comparison to stop: 9 + 6. In fact a number that moves to the front (j becomes -1) needs no extra comparison, as with 2 and 1.
  • DThinks every pair is compared once: 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1. That is the worst case; here the loop stops as soon as it meets a number not larger than x.

Five runners' places

Key point: Case analysis: pick the condition with the fewest possibilities (here A and D together) and try each case, checking it against the other conditions.

A, B, C, D and E run a race and all finish in different places (1st is fastest). D finishes immediately after A; D's and E's places differ by 2 (exactly one runner between them); E is faster than C; C is neither 1st nor last. Who is 3rd?

  1. AA
  2. BE
  3. CD✓
  4. DC

Answer and explanation

Answer: C

A and D take two consecutive places, so try each place for D. D 2nd (A 1st) → E 4th; C can be neither 1st nor 5th, so C is 3rd, but E must beat C. D 3rd (A 2nd) → E 1st or 5th; E 5th cannot beat C, so E is 1st; C cannot be 5th, so C is 4th and B 5th: E A D C B. D 4th (A 3rd) → E 2nd, leaving 1st and 5th for B and C, neither allowed for C. D 5th (A 4th) → E 3rd, and C must come after E, but 4th and 5th are taken. So the order is E, A, D, C, B, and D is 3rd.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • AA is 2nd. If A were 3rd, D would be 4th and E 2nd, leaving 1st and 5th for B and C, but C can be neither.
  • BReads "places differ by 2" as next to each other, giving A D E C B with E 3rd. The question says exactly one runner is between them.
  • DC is 4th. If C were 3rd, E would be 1st or 2nd and A, D would be 4th and 5th; D would be 5th, more than 2 places from E.

Why it is tested

A medium question with 4 cases. A program listed all 120 orders and confirmed only E A D C B fits every condition; reading "differ by 2" as adjacent leaves only A D E C B.

Around a circle: sums of neighbours

Key point: For "how small can the largest value be": find a reason it cannot be smaller (a lower bound), then build an example that reaches it; you need both.

Arrange 1 to 10 around a circle and add each pair of neighbours (10 sums in all). If we want the largest of these sums to be as small as possible, what is the smallest it can be?

  1. A12✓
  2. B11
  3. C13
  4. D15

Answer and explanation

Answer: A

First a lower bound: 10 has two different neighbours, at best 1 and 2, so some sum is at least 10 + 2 = 12. Then a construction: 2, 10, 1, 9, 3, 8, 4, 7, 5, 6 (with 6 next to 2) gives the sums 12, 11, 10, 12, 11, 12, 11, 12, 11, 8, whose largest is 12. With both, the answer is 12.

What is wrong with each wrong option

  • BThinks only of putting 1 next to 10, forgetting that 10 has two neighbours on a circle; the other is at least 2, so the largest sum is at least 12.
  • CAssumes 9 and 10 need four different neighbours (at best 1, 2, 3, 4), so at least 9 + 4 = 13. But 9 and 10 can share the neighbour 1, as in …, 10, 1, 9, ….
  • DUses the alternating order 10, 1, 9, 2, 8, 3, 7, 4, 6, 5, but on a circle the ends 5 and 10 are also neighbours, summing to 15. On a circle, check where the ends meet.

    s += x;
}

A:

Answer and explanation

Answer: A: 7085

x goes 3 → 0 → 1 → 2 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 0 → … (for example 7 × 7 + 1 = 50 and 50 % 10 = 0).     int mid = (lo + hi) / 2;
    cnt++;
    if (mid == t) break;
    else if (mid < t) lo = mid + 1;
    else hi = mid - 1;

Palindromic numbers

Key point: A palindrome is fixed by its first half: there are 9 × 10k − 1 palindromes with n digits (k is n ÷ 2 rounded up), and they are ordered by their first half.

A positive integer is a palindrome if it reads the same from left to right as from right to left, for example 7, 44, 2112 and 10301. (a) The smallest palindrome greater than 2026 is G1, and the largest palindrome less than 2026 is G2. Find G1 and G2. (b) There are H1 palindromes from 1 to 9999. Separately, listing all positive palindromes (not only those up to 9999) from smallest to largest, the 2026th is H2. Find H1 and H2.

Write one integer in each box. G (1.5 marks) needs both G1 and G2 right; H (1.5 marks) needs both H1 and H2 right.

G1:G2:H1:H2:

Answer and explanation

Answer: G1: 2112; G2: 2002; H1: 198; H2: 1027201

(a) Four-digit palindromes have the form abba (a not 0). With a = 2 they are 2002, 2112, 2222, … in order, so G1 = 2112 and G2 = 2002 (the next smaller is 1991). (b) A palindrome is fixed by its first half: there are 9 with one digit (1 to 9), 9 with two (11 to 99), 90 with three (aba: 9 choices for a, 10 for b) and 90 with four (abba), so H1 = 9 + 9 + 90 + 90 = 198. There are 900 each with five digits (abcba) and six digits (abccba), 198 + 900 + 900 = 1998 so far, so the 2026th is the 2026 − 1998 = 28th seven-digit palindrome. A seven-digit palindrome is fixed by its first four digits (1000 to 9999), and smaller first digits give a smaller number; the 28th has first four digits 1000 + 27 = 1027, so H2 = 1027201.

Common wrong answers

  • G2 = 1991: Misses 2002. Four-digit palindromes have the form abba, so 2002 is one, and it is below 2026.
  • H1 = 189: Leaves out the one-digit numbers 1 to 9, which read the same both ways and so are palindromes.
  • H2 = 1028201: Off by one: the 28th seven-digit palindrome has first four digits 1000 + 27 = 1027 (the 1st is 1000), not 1028.

Why it is tested

Systematic counting appeared in 12 of 16 years (bank item M09); the 2025/26 Junior Paper 1 Section C (G, H) also asked for the next number with a given property.

Notes for teachers

G is an easy-to-medium "next number with a property" question, and H needs systematic counting. The answers were checked by listing every palindrome; the wrong answers come from missing 2002, missing one-digit numbers and an off-by-one.

Q18Junior and SeniorFill in3 marks · suggested 6:00

Paying greedily

Key point: Greedy does not always give the fewest; to count the counterexamples, try small amounts to find a pattern, then explain why the pattern holds.

A place has only 1-dollar, 3-dollar and 4-dollar coins. To pay n dollars, the greedy method always uses the largest coin that does not exceed the amount left, until it is paid. For example, for 7 dollars it uses 4, leaving 3, then 3: 2 coins. (a) The greedy method pays 18 dollars with I coins. Find I. (b) The fewest coins that make 18 dollars is J1; and for J2 of the amounts from 1 to 100 dollars, the greedy method does not use the fewest coins. Find J1 and J2.

Write one integer in each box. J (2 marks) needs both J1 and J2 right.

I:J1:J2:

Answer and explanation

Answer: I: 6; J1: 5; J2: 24

(a) 18 = 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 1 + 1, so greedy uses 6 coins: I = 6. (b) 18 = 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 uses only 5 coins, and 4 coins make at most 16 dollars, so J1 = 5. Try small amounts and look for a pattern. Write n = 4q + r with r the remainder when n is divided by 4. Greedy uses q 4-dollar coins and then pays r: nothing for r = 0, one 1 for r = 1, one 3 for r = 3, and two 1s for r = 2. Since q coins or fewer make at most 4q dollars, q + 1 coins is already the fewest for r = 1 and 3, and q is the fewest for r = 0. Only when r = 2 and q ≥ 1 can we do better: replace one 4 and two 1s (6 dollars) by two 3s, using q + 1 coins. So the bad amounts are 6, 10, 14, …, 98 (remainder 2 when divided by 4, and at least 6), (98 − 6) ÷ 4 + 1 = 24 of them: J2 = 24.

Common wrong answers

  • I = 5: This is the fewest coins, not the greedy result. Greedy uses four 4-dollar coins (16), then the 2 left needs two 1-dollar coins: 6 coins.
  • J2 = 25: Also counts 2 dollars, where greedy's 1 + 1 is already the fewest; the bad amounts start at 6 and come every 4 dollars: 6, 10, …, 98, which is 24 amounts.
  • J2 = 1: Finds only the counterexample 6. But 10, 14, 18 and so on all fail: swapping one 4 and two 1s for two 3s saves a coin.

Why it is tested

Trying small cases to find a pattern appeared in 7 of 16 years (bank item M22), with 3 questions in 2025/26; "fewest or most" questions appeared in 11 (M26).

Notes for teachers

I is easy (follow the rule), while J needs a pattern and a reason. The answers were checked by computing, for every amount from 1 to 100, the fewest coins (dynamic programming) and the greedy count; the bad amounts are exactly 6, 10, …, 98.
